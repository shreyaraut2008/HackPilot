## 🌐 About HackPilot

HackPilot is an **AI Operating System purpose-built for hackathons** — not a generic idea generator, not a bland chatbot wrapper, not another productivity tool with a "hackathon mode."

It is a **complete execution engine** that takes your problem statement as input and produces a full winning strategy as output.

### Why HackPilot Exists

Every hackathon has two kinds of participants:

| Type | Behavior | Outcome |
|------|----------|---------|
| **Winging It** | Brainstorms for hours, scopes chaotically, pitches poorly | Top 50% |
| **Strategic** | Scopes smart, builds fast, pitches with precision | Winners |

HackPilot converts the first type into the second — instantly.

### Who It's For

- 🎓 **Students** competing in university and national hackathons
- 🧑‍💻 **Developers** who want to ship fast without the planning fog
- 🏢 **Corporate hackathon teams** who need structured output quickly
- 🌍 **Solo hackers** who need a full co-pilot to fill team gaps
- 🧩 **Repeat hackathon competitors** who want a systematic edge

### What Makes It Different

> Other AI tools answer questions. HackPilot **builds your entire game plan.**

- It doesn't just generate ideas — it evaluates them against **real execution constraints**
- It doesn't just explain tech stacks — it **recommends the right one** for your problem
- It doesn't just outline pitches — it **simulates judge scoring** so you can prepare
- It doesn't just list tasks — it **creates sprint timelines** with realistic time-boxing
- It is designed specifically for the **36–48 hour hackathon context**, not general product development

## 🚀 Core Features

### 🧩 Problem Statement Analyzer

> *"Understand the problem before you solve it."*

Most teams misread the problem statement. HackPilot doesn't.

The **Problem Statement Analyzer** deep-reads your challenge, extracts the core pain points, identifies the target user, surfaces unstated constraints, and maps the solution space — so your entire strategy is anchored to what actually matters.

**Why it matters:** Teams that build the right thing poorly beat teams that build the wrong thing perfectly. Every time.

---

### 💡 Idea Engine

> *"Not just what's possible — what's winnable."*

HackPilot generates multiple optimal project proposals tuned specifically to:

- Your problem domain
- Your stated tech stack preferences
- Hackathon time constraints
- Judge evaluation criteria

Each idea comes with a **viability score**, an **innovation score**, and a plain-English summary so your team can align in minutes, not hours.

**Why it matters:** The Idea Engine kills the brainstorming spiral. You evaluate, not generate — and move on.

---

### ⚖️ Judge Simulator

> *"Win the room before you enter it."*

Judges aren't generic. They care about different things depending on the hackathon type — innovation, technical depth, business viability, social impact, or demo polish.

The **Judge Simulator** analyzes your project concept against likely judge personas and surfaces:

- What will impress them
- What will concern them
- What questions they're likely to ask
- How to pre-empt objections in your pitch

**Why it matters:** Most teams prepare for a pitch. The best teams prepare for *their specific judges*. Now you can too.

---

### 🗺️ Sprint Blueprint (Gantt Timeline)

> *"A plan that fits in 36 hours, not 36 weeks."*

The **Sprint Blueprint** generates a realistic, time-boxed Gantt-style execution roadmap that accounts for:

- Your team size and composition
- Your chosen tech stack
- Required milestones (MVP, demo-ready, pitch-ready)
- Buffer for integration, testing, and the inevitable 3 AM debugging session

**Why it matters:** Unplanned hackathons end in unfinished projects. A sprint blueprint turns a chaotic sprint into a controlled one.

---

### 🎯 Mission Control (Priority Matrix)

> *"Build what matters. Cut everything else."*

The **Mission Control** feature generates a priority matrix that separates:

- **Must Have** — Core features that define your MVP
- **Should Have** — Enhancements that improve demo quality
- **Could Have** — Nice-to-haves if time permits
- **Won't Have** — Scope you explicitly agree to skip

**Why it matters:** Scope creep kills more hackathon projects than skill gaps. Mission Control is your scope firewall.

---

### 🛡️ Scope Guardrail

> *"Ship a great MVP. Not a broken product."*

The **Scope Guardrail** actively monitors your plan for over-engineering red flags. It detects when your proposed solution exceeds what's realistically achievable in the time available, and recommends a trimmed, shippable version.

**Why it matters:** A polished MVP demo beats an ambitious, half-built product on stage. Every. Single. Time.

---

### 🏗️ System Architecture Generator

> *"Don't sketch. Generate."*

The **System Architecture Generator** produces a clean, visual architecture diagram for your project — complete with:

- Frontend / Backend / Database layers
- API integration points
- Data flow between components
- Authentication boundaries
- Third-party service touchpoints

**Why it matters:** A system diagram in your deck signals engineering maturity. It tells judges you thought beyond the demo.

---

### 🎤 Pitch Commander

> *"Your story, structured to win."*

The **Pitch Commander** generates a complete pitch deck outline structured around the winning formula:

1. The Problem (make them feel it)
2. The Solution (make them see it)
3. The Tech (make them trust it)
4. The Demo Hook (make them want it)
5. The Impact (make them care)
6. The Ask / CTA (make them act)

Each slide comes with suggested talking points, timing guidance, and visual cues.

**Why it matters:** The best product with the worst pitch loses. The Pitch Commander closes that gap.

---

### 📊 Win Probability Engine

> *"Know your odds. Improve them."*

The **Win Probability Engine** scores your current project plan against a weighted model of hackathon success factors including:

- Idea novelty and relevance
- Technical feasibility within timeline
- Demo-ability
- Pitch strength estimate
- Judge alignment score

It then provides **specific, actionable recommendations** to improve your score before you start building.

**Why it matters:** Data beats gut feeling. Know where you stand. Fix what you can. Win.

---

### 📤 Export Center

> *"Everything you need, ready to share."*

The **Export Center** packages your entire HackPilot-generated strategy into shareable formats:

- 📋 Markdown plan (for GitHub / Notion)
- 🖼️ Architecture diagram (PNG / SVG)
- 📊 Pitch deck outline (Slide-ready format)
- 🗂️ Sprint timeline export
- 📁 Full project brief (PDF-ready)

**Why it matters:** Share the plan with your team in one click. No reformatting. No copy-pasting. Just execution.

## ⚙️ Installation Guide

### Prerequisites

Make sure you have the following installed before you begin:

- **Node.js** `>=18.0.0` — [Download here](https://nodejs.org)
- **npm** `>=9.0.0` or **pnpm** `>=8.0.0` (recommended)
- **Git** — [Download here](https://git-scm.com)
- An **OpenAI API key** — [Get one here](https://platform.openai.com/api-keys)

---

### Step 1 — Clone the Repository

```bash
git clone https://github.com/your-username/hack-pilot.git
cd hack-pilot
```

---

### Step 2 — Install Dependencies

Using npm:
```bash
npm install
```

Using pnpm (recommended for speed):
```bash
pnpm install
```

---

### Step 3 — Configure Environment Variables

Create a `.env.local` file in the root of the project:

```bash
cp .env.example .env.local
```

Open `.env.local` and fill in your values:

```env
# OpenAI — Required for all AI features
OPENAI_API_KEY=sk-...your-key-here...

# App URL — Used for metadata and OG images
NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL=http://localhost:3000

# Optional: Analytics
NEXT_PUBLIC_POSTHOG_KEY=phc_...
NEXT_PUBLIC_POSTHOG_HOST=https://app.posthog.com
```

> ⚠️ **Never commit your `.env.local` file.** It is already in `.gitignore`.

---

### Step 4 — Run the Development Server

```bash
npm run dev
# or
pnpm dev
```

Open [http://localhost:3000](http://localhost:3000) in your browser.

You should see the HackPilot terminal interface. If you see a blank screen, check your terminal for errors.

---

### Step 5 — Build for Production

```bash
npm run build
```

This will create an optimized production build in the `.next` directory. Verify there are no build errors before deploying.

---

### Step 6 — Run Production Server Locally

```bash
npm run start
```

---

### Step 7 — Deploy to Vercel (Recommended)

```bash
# Install Vercel CLI
npm install -g vercel

# Deploy
vercel

# Set environment variables in Vercel dashboard or via CLI
vercel env add OPENAI_API_KEY
```

Or click below for one-click deploy:

[![Deploy with Vercel](https://vercel.com/button)](https://vercel.com/new/clone?repository-url=https://github.com/your-username/hack-pilot)

## 🔐 Environment Variables Reference

| Variable | Required | Description |

## 📖 Usage Guide

### Basic Usage

1. Open HackPilot at [https://hack-pilot-murex.vercel.app/](https://hack-pilot-murex.vercel.app/)
2. Paste your hackathon problem statement into the terminal input
3. Click **"Forge Co-Pilot Bundle"** or press `Ctrl + Enter`
4. Wait ~10–15 seconds while the AI engine processes
5. Review your complete execution blueprint

## 🧭 Development Philosophy

### ⚡ Speed Is a Feature

In a hackathon, time is the one resource you can't recover. HackPilot's entire UX is built around the idea that **planning time should be measured in minutes, not hours**. Every interaction is optimized for speed of insight — from the single-input interface to the one-click export. If it takes more than 3 clicks to get value, we rethink the flow.

### 🎯 Execution Beats Brainstorming

The hackathon mythology glorifies the eureka moment — the brilliant idea that changes everything. In reality, **disciplined execution of a good idea** outperforms scattered work on a great one. HackPilot is built on this conviction. Every feature pushes you toward doing, not just thinking.

### 🚢 Ship a Great MVP, Not a Broken Product

Scope is the silent killer. HackPilot's Scope Guardrail and Priority Matrix exist because the hardest thing in a hackathon isn't building — it's **deciding what not to build**. We optimize for a polished, demo-ready MVP every time, because that's what wins.

### 🎤 Pitch Is Product

Technical excellence without communication is invisible. The Pitch Commander and Judge Simulator exist because we believe **the ability to tell your story clearly is as important as building the thing**. The best teams at every hackathon are the best storytellers.

### 🔍 Transparency Over Magic

HackPilot shows you its reasoning. Every score, every recommendation, every timeline comes with an explanation. We don't hide decisions behind a black box — we help you understand why, so you can adapt and improve.

## 🤝 Contributing

Contributions are what make open source incredible. We welcome them warmly.

### How to Contribute

1. **Fork** the repository
2. **Create** a feature branch: `git checkout -b feature/amazing-feature`
3. **Commit** your changes: `git commit -m 'feat: add amazing feature'`
4. **Push** to your branch: `git push origin feature/amazing-feature`
5. **Open** a Pull Request

### Contribution Guidelines

- Follow the existing code style (TypeScript strict mode, ESLint, Prettier)
- Write descriptive commit messages (we follow [Conventional Commits](https://www.conventionalcommits.org))
- Add tests for new features where applicable
- Update documentation if you change behavior
- Be kind and constructive in code reviews — we're all builders here

### Good First Issues

Look for issues tagged `good first issue` or `help wanted` — these are great starting points if you're new to the codebase.

### Reporting Bugs

Found something broken? Open an issue with:
- What you expected to happen
- What actually happened
- Steps to reproduce
- Your OS, browser, and Node version
